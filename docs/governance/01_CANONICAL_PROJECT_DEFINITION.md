# Canonical Project Definition

**Document ID:** OBDIA-CANON-001  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Approved Baseline  
**Authority:** Project Founder  
**Change Control:** Changes require explicit founder approval.

---

The Officer-Bound Digital Investigation Agent is not an autonomous police officer and does not possess independent legal authority.

It is an official, institutionally issued and cryptographically bound digital agent that operates exclusively as a delegated professional extension of an identified human law-enforcement officer.

The agent may support authorized investigative activities across heterogeneous digital environments, including:

- the public Surface Web;
- authenticated and non-indexed Internet services commonly classified as part of the Deep Web;
- legally authorized and operationally isolated Dark Web environments and anonymity networks;
- Web3 ecosystems, including blockchains, smart contracts, wallets, decentralized applications and decentralized storage;
- cloud platforms;
- public and institutionally authorized APIs;
- communication platforms;
- digital evidence repositories;
- cybersecurity and threat-intelligence systems.

The agent must never receive unrestricted or general investigative authority.

Every operation must be bound to:

- an identified human officer;
- an issuing institution;
- an authorized case or operational mandate;
- a declared investigative purpose;
- a defined jurisdiction;
- a restricted set of data sources and tools;
- explicit permissions;
- a fixed validity period;
- technical and legal policy controls;
- complete auditability;
- chain-of-custody requirements;
- human responsibility and review.

The system must distinguish between:

1. publicly accessible information;
2. institutionally authorized access;
3. access requiring additional legal authorization;
4. prohibited or out-of-scope actions.

No component may be designed to facilitate unauthorized access, indiscriminate surveillance, unlawful deanonymization, offensive intrusion, uncontrolled interaction with criminal infrastructure or autonomous coercive decision-making.

The architecture must prioritize:

- human accountability;
- workload and agent identity;
- scoped delegation;
- least privilege;
- Zero Trust;
- purpose limitation;
- data minimization;
- privacy and fundamental rights;
- evidence provenance;
- chain of custody;
- immutable auditability;
- operational isolation;
- secure tool use;
- prompt-injection resistance;
- revocation and emergency containment;
- reproducibility of investigative actions.

The project must clearly separate:

- established facts;
- existing technologies;
- proposed architecture;
- experimental implementation;
- simulated capabilities;
- future research hypotheses.

All demonstrations must use synthetic data, authorized test environments, public lawful sources, local simulations, testnets or controlled cybersecurity laboratories.
